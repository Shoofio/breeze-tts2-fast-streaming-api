# Breeze MLX frame-loop prototype: results

The machine is an Apple M5 with 16 GB, running macOS 26.6.2, mlx 0.32.3 and mlx-audio e1b19b9 (unmodified). One model process ran at a time. Swap was about 3.5 GB used before and after the chain. A browser and an editor were open, so the numbers are not from a quiet machine.

## Scripts

| File | Purpose |
|---|---|
| `common.py` | Paths, texts, the WAV writer, and `make_fp32()`, a diagnostic that switches activations to float32 on the in-memory model |
| `profile_stock.py` | Task 1. Runs stock `generate()` unchanged, then a faithful copy of its loop with `mx.eval` barriers for attribution, plus graph-node counts |
| `proto.py` | Task 2. Levels `stock,a,b,c,d,e` (cumulative), `--cfg`, `--greedy` (top_k=1), `--fp32`, `--depth-bits N`, `--wav`. Saves the frames to `frames/` |
| `bench_components.py` | Synchronous GPU time of each level-d component, compiled and eager |
| `diag_teacher.py` | Teacher-forced check, bug vs numerics. Feeds the stock frames through both paths and compares the logits at every sampling point |
| `diag_depth.py`, `cmp.py` | Per-step depth logit diff, and the first frame where two frame files diverge |
| `requant.py` | Speed probe: requantizes the depth decoder to 4-bit affine |
| `run_all.sh` | The benchmark chain. Raw output is in `results/*.jsonl` |

## Benchmark text, and whether the gate passage hit the cap

- **Gate passage (README "What this is", 1219 chars):** stock 8-bit without CFG produced **750 frames with no EOS**. It hit `max_tokens`, so the Phase-0 numbers came from a capped run. The per-frame cost (114 ms) is still valid.
- **Benchmark passage:** I used `BENCH_PASSAGE` in `common.py`, a 444-character lighthouse-keeper paragraph. Stock reaches EOS naturally: 377 frames / 30.2 s without CFG, 376 frames / 30.1 s with CFG. Every prototype run below also ended on EOS, at 333–424 frames.
- All runs use one segment (`split_pattern=None`), seed 42, and stock sampling (temp 0.9, top_p 1.0, top_k 50, no repetition penalty). The codec runs every 2 frames. Each level is warmed up on the same text before it is timed.

## 1. Profile of the stock loop (8-bit, passage, `mx.eval` barriers)

| ms/frame | backbone step | depth decoder fwd (15 cb) | sampling (16 × `.item()`) | codec (per frame) | other | total (barriered) | stock, no barriers |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| no CFG | 13.1 | **92.3** | 4.5 | 7.0 | ~0 | 116.9 | 114.0 |
| CFG 4 | 23.8 | **174.3** | 4.4 | 7.1 | ~0 | 209.5 | 207.4 |

- **The depth decoder takes about 80% of the frame.** It has **no KV cache**: `_DepthDecoder.next_logits` runs all 12 layers again over the whole growing sequence (length 2..16) for each of the 15 codebooks. That works out to 6.2 ms per depth step, 12.4 ms with CFG.
- **Host syncs per frame (stock):** 16 `int(token.item())` calls in `_sample` (1 for codebook 0, 15 for depth), plus 0.5 codec `mx.eval` (once per 2 frames), giving **16.5 blocking syncs per frame**. CFG does not add syncs, but it doubles every forward pass because the two branches run one after the other. Repetition penalty is off by default, so no Python list work happens per step.
- **Lazy-graph size** (primitives in `mx.export_to_dot`, views included, so this is an upper bound on kernels): the backbone step is 828 and one depth step (at length 4) is 300. That is about 5,500 primitives per frame without CFG and about 11,000 with CFG.
- Sampling and Python overhead are small (~4.5 ms). The stock loop is GPU-bound on the depth decoder's re-run.

## 2. Cumulative optimisations (passage, stock sampling)

Levels: **a** adds a depth KV cache (one token per step after a 2-token start). **b** adds CFG as batch 2 through the backbone and the depth decoder; the backbone uses a fixed buffer with left padding and per-row RoPE offsets, so positions match stock. **c** removes the per-token `.item()` calls: the frame is one lazy graph, frame n+1 is queued with `async_eval` before frame n's EOS flag is read (one speculative frame), and the codec runs every 2 frames with no codes read back to the host. That leaves one host sync per frame. **d** adds `mx.compile` of the whole frame step (head sampling, 15 depth steps and the backbone step) over a fixed-capacity KV buffer (rounded up to multiples of 256 slots) so shapes never change. **e** adds the codec on a second GPU stream.

### Component times at level d (`bench_components.py`, synchronous, 8-bit)

| component | eager | compiled | CFG compiled |
|---|---|---|---|
| head sampling + 15 depth steps | 47.7 ms | 46.5 ms | 47.2 ms |
| backbone step (1024-slot buffer) | 14.5 ms | 14.4 ms | 16.6 ms |
| codec, per frame (2-frame step / 2) | 6.9 ms | – | – |
| depth with 4-bit requant | 30.1 ms | 29.0 ms | 29.8 ms |

These add up to about 68 ms, which matches the 64–68 ms measured end to end. The prototype is now **GPU-bound on weight bandwidth**:

- One depth step reads about 0.35 GB at mxfp8 (12 layers × 27.8M params). 15 steps is about 5.2 GB per frame. 46.5 ms means about 112 GB/s.
- The backbone reads about 1.45 GB at about 100 GB/s.
- Halving the depth decoder's bits cut its time from 46.5 to 29 ms, which confirms the bandwidth limit.

This also explains why bf16 cannot reach real time: about 10.4 GB per frame gives a floor near 70–100 ms before the codec is counted.

## 3. Correctness

**Greedy free-running (top_k=1, no repetition penalty, 8-bit, sentence), stock vs prototype, first divergence:**

| case | first diverging frame / codebook | stock frames | proto frames |
|---|---|---|---|
| no CFG, bf16 activations | frame 0, cb 12 | 76 (EOS) | 750 (greedy loops on token 706 until the cap) |
| CFG, bf16 activations | frame 0, cb 9 (a) / cb 10 (b, d) | 81 (EOS) | 78–79 (EOS) |
| no CFG, fp32 activations | frame 8, cb 8 | 116 (EOS) | 74 (EOS) |
| CFG, fp32 activations | frame 1, cb 2 | 114 (EOS) | 85 (EOS) |

So **the target of identical frames for the first 50 frames was not met.** Levels a, b, c and d all produce identical frames to each other. The divergence is the depth KV cache against the stock full re-run.

**Why this is numerics and not a bug (teacher-forced, `diag_teacher.py`).** Stock's own greedy frames were fed through both paths, and the CFG-combined logits were compared at all 16 sampling points per frame:

| case | points | argmax mismatches | max ref top-2 margin among mismatches | median / max abs logit error | EOS predicted at the same step |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| no CFG, bf16 | 1216 | 18 | (all within error) | – / 0.25 | yes |
| CFG, bf16 | 1296 | 111 | (all within error) | – / 1.39 | yes |
| no CFG, fp32 | 1856 | **1** | **0.00016** | 0.0015 / 0.032 | yes |
| CFG, fp32 | 1824 | **5** | **0.0068** | 0.0104 / 0.044 | yes |

- In bf16, the logit error is 1–2 ULPs. Example: frame 0, cb 12 has a stock top-2 margin of 0.031 and an error of 0.095.
- CFG multiplies the error by about 4 (4c − 3u), so CFG mismatches are more frequent.
- In fp32, every mismatch is a near-exact tie (margin ≤ 0.007), and the rest agree. No mismatch has a large margin.
- The cause is different kernels: quantized matmul with M=1 (qmv) against M=2..16 (qmm), and differently ordered reductions. Exact token parity with a KV cache is not attainable. Teacher-forced agreement is the meaningful check.
- Under sampling, all prototype runs end on EOS at lengths similar to stock (8-bit passage: 382–424 vs 376–377 frames).

**WAVs to listen to** (sentence, stock sampling, seed 42) are in `wav/`:
- `8bit_sentence_stock_nocfg_sample.wav` / `8bit_sentence_d_nocfg_sample.wav`
- `8bit_sentence_stock_cfg_sample.wav` / `8bit_sentence_d_cfg_sample.wav`
- also `8bit_passage_e_cfg_sample_depth4bit.wav` (the 4-bit depth probe)

The audio of the two paths differs because the sampled tokens diverge. Judge them by quality, not by sample-for-sample identity.

## What failed or did not help, and why

- **mx.compile (c→d): about 0.5–1 ms per frame.** Once the syncs were gone, graph building already overlapped GPU work through `async_eval`. The GPU is bandwidth-bound, so fusing elementwise ops saves little. No recompiles occurred, because the fixed-capacity buffer keeps shapes constant; capacity is rounded up in steps of 256 so one trace serves many texts. Tracing cost under 1 s (`warm_s`).
- **Codec on a second stream (e): about 1 ms per frame,** inside run-to-run noise.
- **The fixed buffer costs a little.** Attending over the full 512–1024-slot buffer reads about 0.1 GB of KV per row per step. That is part of why CFG is about 2 ms slower than no CFG.
- **Exact greedy parity with stock** was not achieved; see above. It is not achievable once the depth cache changes kernel shapes.
- **First audio got slightly worse in c–e** (0.30 vs 0.22–0.25 s without CFG, 0.42 vs 0.34 s with CFG). The speculative next frame is queued ahead of the first codec chunk. All values are still well under 1 s.
- **Not measured:** an ablation of b, c and d in a different order; the gate passage at the new levels; bf16 at levels a–c; repeated runs for variance; and 4-bit depth quality.

## Assessment

- **RTF ≤ 1.0 at 8-bit with CFG is reachable on this machine.** The prototype measured **RTF 0.829–0.843** with CFG (66–68 ms/frame) and 0.81–0.82 without. Real-time margin is about 15–17%.
- **Nearly all of the win comes from two changes:** the depth KV cache (a) and batched CFG (b). Sync removal (c) adds about 4–5 ms. Compile and the codec stream add about 1 ms each.
- **bf16 cannot reach RTF 1.0**, even with all changes: 115–117 ms/frame, because weight bandwidth sets the floor.
- **The next lever is depth-decoder weight bandwidth.** The depth decoder reads about 5 GB per frame, now about 70% of the frame. A lower-bit depth decoder took the frame to about 47–49 ms (RTF about 0.6, including CFG), but its quality has to be checked by listening or a metric before it is used.
- **Smaller levers:**
  - Attend only over the live slots, using a few capacity buckets instead of one large one.
  - A lower-bit backbone, which is about 14 ms.
  - Keeping the codec's ~7 ms per frame off the critical path. A second stream did not help, so this would need different scheduling.
